💡 GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY
A few words in this lesson are worth a look before you read. They show up in bold, and you can hover over any of them for a reminder.
colony (KAH-luh-nee). A place settled and controlled by people from a faraway country.
convert (kun-VURT). To change from one religion or belief to another.
displace (dis-PLAYS). To force people to leave the place where they live.
empire (EM-pyr). A large area of land and many peoples ruled by one government.
intermarry (in-ter-MAIR-ee). To marry someone from a different group or culture.
settler (SET-ler). A person who moves to a new place to live there for good.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Same problem. Three different answers.
Here is the problem European explorers faced. People already lived in the Americas, and three European empires wanted the land. In this lesson, you will see how each one tried to solve it. Each answer is called a model, which is a pattern that a country follows again and again. Pay attention to what each model needed in order to work.

2

📚 PART 1 · THE SPANISH ANSWER: CONQUER, CONVERT, INCLUDE

Take over, and put people to work

Spain's first answer was to conquer the people who already lived in a place. Spain then asked them to convert to Christianity and made them part of the Spanish system as workers. Spaniards believed they were bringing a good thing, their religion. They also wanted wealth. The people already living there would grow food, dig for silver and gold, and pay taxes. Spanish officials would sit at the top. A place ruled this way was called a colony.

Spain used a paper to say so out loud. It was called the Requirement (Requerimiento). Spanish officers read it to Native people, telling them to accept the Spanish king and the Church, or war would follow. Often it was read in Spanish to people who could not understand a word.

🌐 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
the Requirement (Requerimiento) (reh-keh-ree-mee-EN-toh). A Spanish paper from 1513 that was read aloud to Native peoples. It told them to accept the Spanish king and the Christian faith.

Notice what this model needed. Only a small number of Spaniards crossed the ocean. They did not plan to do the work themselves. They planned to rule over many people who would do it. The Spanish model worked best where a large population already lived and already knew how to farm and pay taxes to a ruler.

For Spain, the model paid off. In the 1500s, huge amounts of silver from Spanish mines flowed back across the ocean, and it helped make Spain one of the richest and most powerful kingdoms in Europe. But Spaniards were always a small minority in their own colonies. The system depended on the labor of the people they ruled.

📚 PART 2 · THE FRENCH ANSWER: TRADE, ALLY, INTERMARRY

Few settlers, many friends

France sent very few settlers. French traders built posts along the St. Lawrence River and traded with Native peoples for furs, especially beaver. Beaver fur was in high demand in Europe, where it was made into hats. France built a small town at Quebec in 1608, but it stayed small for a long time.

The French needed Native hunters and trappers to bring in the furs. So they did not try to rule them. They made alliances instead. Many French traders learned Native languages, and some intermarried with Native families. Their children grew up in both worlds. Allies also fought on the same side when trouble came.

French priests also traveled to Native villages to share their faith. They often lived there as guests, not as rulers. For a long time, New France had only a few thousand French settlers, while the English colonies grew to tens of thousands.

This model needed very few French people. What it needed most was friends. It worked best in a land of forests and rivers, where Native hunters knew the animals and the trails, and there were no big farming populations to tax.

📚 PART 3 · THE ENGLISH ANSWER: MOVE IN, KEEP APART, PUSH OUT

Whole communities cross the ocean

England tried something different. English settlers came in families, with men, women, and children, and they planned to stay. They built towns and farms that looked like the ones they had left. Jamestown in Virginia began in 1607. Plymouth in Massachusetts began in 1620.

English settlers wanted farmland more than they wanted Native workers or furs. They kept their own towns separate from Native villages. As their towns grew, they needed more land, and Native peoples were often displaced. There was trade and some friendship, but there was also a lot of conflict over who controlled the land.

English settlers also brought their ideas about government. They set up town meetings and elected assemblies, and they expected to run their own local affairs under the king. They believed that a settler who owned land had a say in how the colony was run.

This model needed many settlers and a lot of open farmland. It worked best in a place with good soil and a climate like England's.

📚 PART 4 · AN ANSWER IS TIED TO A PLACE

No model is best everywhere

Look at the three models side by side. Each one was an answer to a problem, and each fit a certain kind of place. In central Mexico, millions of farmers already paid tribute to rulers, and the Spanish model could take that system over. In the northern forests, there were furs to trade and few farmers to tax, and the French model fit. On the eastern coast, there was good farmland and a growing number of English settlers, and the English model fit.

Another way to see the difference is to ask what each power wanted the land to give it. Spain wanted workers and silver. France wanted furs and allies who could supply them. England wanted ground to plow and room to grow. Because they wanted different things, each one was good at different kinds of places.

None of these answers was bound to win. Each one worked until it reached a place it did not fit. That is why the next question matters so much. Texas is not central Mexico. It is not the northern forest. It is not the English coast. So which model reaches Texas, and why that one?

Before you go on, write your guess in your notebook, along with one reason. Keep it, and check it again later. The question underneath it is a big one: who was going to own this place?

💪 WHAT TO CARRY FORWARD

You now have a set of three models to test. Spain conquers and rules. France trades and allies. England moves in and holds land apart. The Spanish model reached its greatest success in one of the largest cities in North America.
