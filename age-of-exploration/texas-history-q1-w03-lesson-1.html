💡 GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY
One name is worth reviewing before you read.
Karankawa (kuh-RAN-kuh-wah) — a Native American people who lived along the Texas Gulf Coast, moving with the seasons between the coastline and inland areas.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What happens when the conquest model shows up shipwrecked, starving, and outnumbered?
In 1528, a Spanish expedition under Pánfilo de Narváez fell apart on the Texas coast. The men who survived did not arrive as conquerors. They arrived as castaways, and the people who found them held every advantage the Spanish had held in Mexico. Nothing about the conquest playbook applies here, starting now.

📚 PART 1 · AN EXPEDITION FALLS APART
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read on: if your ship wrecked on an unfamiliar coast with no supplies, what would worry you most? (No wrong answer — just check in with yourself.)

The Narváez expedition of 1528 set out to explore and claim the Gulf coast. It went badly almost immediately: ships were lost, supplies ran out, and the surviving men were reduced to building crude rafts out of horsehide just to keep moving along the coast. In November 1528, what was left of the expedition washed up on the Texas shore, exhausted and starving.

📚 PART 2 · THE REVERSAL

Who the Karankawa were

The Karankawa lived along this stretch of coast, moving with the seasons to make use of shellfish beds, fish runs, and inland game as each became available. Their way of life depended on careful, seasonal use of a coastline that could not support unlimited extra mouths to feed.

Fed, then enslaved

At first, the Karankawa fed the shipwrecked Spaniards. This was not weakness or naivety: it was a real response to people in genuine distress. But as the Spaniards' numbers stayed high and their usefulness stayed low, that response changed. The same men who had been fed were increasingly put to hard labor, effectively enslaved by the people who had first taken pity on them.

💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
This reversal is not a story about the Karankawa suddenly turning cruel. It is a story about a resource-limited coastline and a group of outsiders who could not yet contribute anything to it. Their treatment shifted with the real, practical burden they placed on a society with no surplus to spare.
