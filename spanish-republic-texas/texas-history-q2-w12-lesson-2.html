📚 PART 1 · A REPORT, THEN A LAW

In 1828, the Mexican official Manuel de Mier y Terán inspected Texas and reported back to Mexico City. What he found alarmed officials there: Anglo settlement was outpacing Mexican population and control across the province. The Law of April 6, 1830, was Mexico City's direct response. It halted further immigration from the United States and stepped up customs enforcement along the coast.

⚠️ WATCH OUT
Notice the cause and effect here. Mier y Terán's report does not cause a crisis by itself; it is Mexico City's policy response to that report, the Law of April 6, that actually lands on the colonists and starts the confrontation.
📚 PART 2 · ANAHUAC, TURTLE BAYOU, AND THE CONVENTIONS

Enforcement of the new customs rules at the garrison town of Anahuac provoked open resistance in 1832. The colonists involved issued the Turtle Bayou Resolutions, which declared loyalty not to Mexico's centralist government but to the federalists and to General Santa Anna, who was then leading a movement against the same centralizing policies. Notice what this loyalty statement is not: it is not yet a declaration of independence from Mexico.
