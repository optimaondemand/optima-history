📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Six weeks of retreat, for eighteen minutes of battle.
As Houston's army retreated east, civilians fled alongside it in what became known as the Runaway Scrape. Houston absorbed real political cost for that retreat: officials demanding he fight, his own army nearly mutinous. His stated reasoning was simple: preserve the only organized force Texas actually had, until the ground and the timing favored it.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain why Houston's six-week retreat, though politically costly, made tactical sense.
2
I will be able to describe what made the ground at San Jacinto especially advantageous for Houston's army.
3
I will be able to state what allowed Santa Anna's army to be defeated so quickly, in about eighteen minutes.

📚 PART 1 · A COMMANDER CHOOSES HIS GROUND

Santa Anna, meanwhile, divided his own army and outran his supply line chasing Houston east. On April 21, Houston attacked in the afternoon across ground made of timber and marsh, with Vince's Bridge already destroyed on the only road back to the Brazos. The conflict lasted about eighteen minutes.

💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
Six weeks of retreat and eighteen minutes of battle are not opposites. The retreat is what made the eighteen minutes possible: Houston preserved his only force until he could pick ground that denied Santa Anna's usual advantage entirely.
