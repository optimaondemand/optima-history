📚 PART 1 · TWO EXPEDITIONS, TWO DISASTERS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
If a small, cash-poor republic sent an expedition far beyond its own borders to try to claim new territory or trade routes, what do you think the odds of success would be? (No wrong answer — just check in with yourself.)

In 1841, Lamar sent the Santa Fe Expedition to try to open trade with New Mexico and extend Texas's claim there. It was an overreach: Mexican forces captured the expedition, and its survivors were marched as prisoners to Mexico City. The following year, Mexican forces under Vásquez and then Woll briefly seized San Antonio itself. In response, Texans launched the Mier Expedition into Mexico; when it too was captured, imprisoned Texans were forced to draw beans from a jar, black or white, to decide who would be executed, remembered as the black bean lottery.

📚 PART 2 · WHY WASHINGTON WAS WATCHING

Both Britain and France showed real interest in an independent Texas, seeing trade and diplomatic opportunity in a young republic still unrecognized by Mexico. A cotton-growing, slaveholding republic under growing British influence alarmed Washington far more than it alarmed Mexico City, since it touched the United States' own economic and strategic interests directly.

⚠️ WATCH OUT
Do not read this as Mexico simply not caring about Texas. Mexico's concern was recovering lost territory. Washington's concern was different: a rival European power gaining real influence on U.S. soil, right on its own southern border.

A nation that could neither fund itself nor defend its own frontier was running out of options. The next lesson follows exactly how that pressure resolves.
