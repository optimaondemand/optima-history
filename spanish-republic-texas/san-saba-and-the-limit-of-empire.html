📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
One mission, taken by force. One line, formally abandoned.
In 1757, Spain founded a mission to the Lipan Apache on the San Sabá River, past the line where its usual model still worked. In 1758, a Comanche-led coalition destroyed it, the only Texas mission ever taken by force. What follows is Spain admitting, formally, that this frontier has a limit.

📚 PART 1 · THE ONLY MISSION TAKEN BY FORCE

The 1757 mission to the Lipan Apache on the San Sabá River sat on ground the Comanche saw as contested, and their alliance with the Apache was hostile at best. In 1758, a Comanche-led coalition destroyed the mission. Spain's 1759 punitive expedition then failed on the Red River, a real demonstration that Spanish military force could not project effective power against a mounted Comanche coalition on this ground.

⚠️ WATCH OUT
Do not read San Sabá as a random tragedy. It is the direct result of the model in Lesson 7.1: Spain's own gift, the horse, had already tipped the balance of power on this exact ground.
📚 PART 2 · A LINE ON THE RAINFALL MAP

Rubí's 1767 inspection and the New Regulations of 1772 formally abandon East Texas as a defensible line, contracting Spain's actual frontier inward. Try the interactive below to see exactly where this line sits, and why.
