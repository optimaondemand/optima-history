💡 GLOSSARY OF SUBJECT VOCABULARY
One word is worth reviewing before you read. You will see it constantly for the rest of the semester.
empresario (em-preh-SAH-ree-oh) — a person granted the right by the Mexican government to bring a set number of settler families into Texas and organize a colony.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A new government looks at an empty, dangerous north and invites in the one power that can actually fill it.
Mexico's new government inherited exactly what Week 8 described: almost no population in Texas, no meaningful revenue from it, and Comanche raiding reaching deep into the interior. The solution it lands on invites in settlers Mexico cannot easily supply itself, on the theory that a buffer population will fight to defend its own land.

📚 PART 1 · THE INHERITANCE, AND THE BUFFER LOGIC
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read on: if you ran a government with a dangerous, empty frontier and not enough of your own people willing to move there, would you consider inviting in outsiders to settle it? (No wrong answer — just check in with yourself.)

The buffer logic is straightforward: settle loyal colonists on the frontier, and they will have every incentive to defend their own land, doing the work of frontier defense Mexico cannot easily do itself. This invitation actually predates Mexican independence: Moses Austin petitioned the Spanish government for a colonization grant in 1820, before Mexico even existed as an independent nation.

📚 PART 2 · BUILDING THE LEGAL FRAMEWORK

Mexico built the legal framework for foreign colonization gradually, across a series of laws in 1823, 1824, and 1825, each one refining what an empresario could offer settlers and what it required of them in return. No single law resolved the process all at once; it took several successive steps to build the system Stephen Austin would end up operating inside.
