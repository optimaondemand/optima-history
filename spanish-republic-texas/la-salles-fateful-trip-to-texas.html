📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A map error, a lost fleet, and a colony that lasted less than four years.
France built its American empire on furs and friendship. In the 1680s, a French explorer set out to carry that empire to the Gulf of Mexico. He never reached the place he was aiming for. Instead he landed on the coast of Texas, and his mistake changed the history of the whole region.

📚 PART 1 · THE FRENCH MODEL IN PRACTICE

From the St. Lawrence to the Gulf

By now you know how France did things. French traders paid Native peoples for furs, made alliances, and kept their settlements small. The heart of New France was the St. Lawrence River and the Great Lakes. La Salle was an explorer who pushed that system farther west and south. In 1682, he paddled down the Mississippi River to the Gulf of Mexico. He claimed the whole river valley for King Louis XIV and named it Louisiana, in honor of the king.

Nearly all of the wealth of New France came from the fur trade. The French did not try to turn Native peoples into workers or to take over their land. Because they needed skilled trappers, they worked to keep good relations. This worked in the north, where Native hunters knew the forests and rivers, and where a French trader could sail home each year with a boat full of furs.

Then La Salle went back to France with a bold plan. He would plant a colony at the mouth of the Mississippi. From there, France could trade with Native peoples up the river. It could also threaten the silver mines of Spain, France's old rival. The king agreed to help.

🌐 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
La Salle (lah SAHL). Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, a French explorer.

La Salle was a driven and difficult man. He had a talent for big plans, and he could inspire men to follow him. He could also be harsh, proud, and slow to listen. Many of the people who sailed with him did not like him. These traits matter, because a leader's character can decide whether a hard journey succeeds or fails.

📚 PART 2 · A COSTLY MISTAKE

Lost at sea

In 1684, La Salle sailed from France with four ships and about three hundred people. There were soldiers, workers, families, and priests. Trouble started early. La Salle argued with the captain of the navy ship, and sickness spread through the crowded decks. Worse, nobody could find the river.

In those days, sailors could not measure longitude. It tells how far east or west a ship has traveled. Without it, the crew had to guess, and they guessed wrong. They sailed right past the mouth of the Mississippi by hundreds of miles. In February 1685, the ships entered Matagorda Bay on the coast of Texas.

A ship's captain could tell how far north or south he was by looking at the sun and stars. East and west was much harder. Clocks of the day lost time at sea, so the best a sailor could do was guess from his speed and direction. A small mistake grew bigger every day. That is how a fleet aiming for one river ended up hundreds of miles away.

When La Salle first saw the coast, he suspected that his ships had gone too far west. Still, he hoped that one of the rivers flowing into the bay was a branch of the Mississippi. He spent weeks exploring the shore and the bay looking for it. Meanwhile his supplies shrank and his men grew restless.

La Salle chose to stay. He built a small fort near the bay and called it Fort St. Louis. Ships were lost one by one. Spanish pirates captured one on the way across. Another ran aground. A third, called La Belle, sank in 1686. More than three hundred years later, in 1995, archaeologists found La Belle in the mud at the bottom of the bay.

🌐 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
Matagorda (mat-uh-GOR-duh). A large bay on the Texas coast, where La Salle landed in 1685.

Think about the choice La Salle faced. He could have given up and sailed away, but he had spent the king's money and staked his name on this plan. Going home with nothing would have meant shame. Pride and hope may have pushed him to stay, and the colony paid the price.

📚 PART 3 · FORT ST. LOUIS

A fort without friends

The French model needed friends, but La Salle had none on this coast. Early on, French colonists took canoes from the Karankawa, and the two groups began to fight. The colonists also had little food and no farms. Snakes, sickness, and hunger killed many of them. Within two years, only a few dozen were left.

🌐 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
Karankawa (kuh-RANG-kuh-wah). The name often used today for the coastal peoples of the Texas Gulf coast.

The French model of friendship had little chance here. La Salle's party had arrived as strangers with weapons, not as traders with goods. For the Karankawa, the French were people who took canoes and stayed where they were not wanted. They had no reason to treat them as allies.

There were no furs to trade, no allies to trade with, and no French town within hundreds of miles. La Salle decided to walk overland to find the Mississippi and bring help from Canada. In March 1687, somewhere in East Texas, a group of his own men murdered him in a mutiny. His colony never learned what had happened to him.

The few crops the colonists planted did poorly. La Salle made several trips along the coast and inland to look for the Mississippi and for help. Each trip cost him men, to sickness, accident, and fighting.

Life at the fort was miserable. The ground was hard, the water was bad in places, and the men had not planned for a long stay. Some of the colonists died of disease. Others wandered off and were never seen again. Without fresh supplies from France, the little settlement slowly fell apart.

📚 PART 4 · THE END OF THE COLONY

What was left

Back at the fort, the survivors were mostly women and children. In the winter of 1688 to 1689, the Karankawa attacked. They killed most of the people who remained and took some of the children. The French colony in Texas was over in less than four years.

A few men escaped. Joutel was one of them. He had left with a small party that followed La Salle north. They found French outposts on the Mississippi and in Canada, and eventually he reached France. He wrote a journal of the expedition, and it is one of our best sources for what happened.

🌐 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
Joutel (zhoo-TEL). Henri Joutel, a French soldier who survived La Salle's expedition and wrote a journal about it.

The colony failed because the French model needed what this place did not have. It needed Native friends, fur to trade, and a French town nearby. La Salle had none of the three. But the failure did not end the story. Spanish officials in Mexico heard about Fort St. Louis. They saw a French colony on a coast Spain had never settled. They realized they could lose Texas, and that made them act.

💪 WHAT TO CARRY FORWARD

One bad guess about longitude put a French colony in Texas, and the French model fell apart there. But the colony sent a warning to Spain. For more than a hundred years, Spain had ignored Texas. After Fort St. Louis, it could not afford to.
