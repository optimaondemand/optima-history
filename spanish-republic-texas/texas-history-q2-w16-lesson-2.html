📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A country with almost nothing to spend, and one resource it has plenty of.
A nation of roughly 40,000 people generated far too little tax revenue and economic output to cover the costs of running an independent government, let alone defending it. The Republic's paper currency, the redbacks, collapsed in value as a direct result.

📚 PART 1 · A CURRENCY NO ONE TRUSTS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
If a government prints paper money but has almost no gold, silver, or steady tax income backing it, what do you think happens to that money's value over time? (No wrong answer — just check in with yourself.)

The Republic's debt piled up quickly, and its paper currency, known as redbacks, collapsed to pennies on the dollar. The government's paper currency lost public and international confidence, reflecting severe underlying financial weakness rather than a temporary dip. A population this small simply could not generate the tax revenue or economic output to cover the costs of an independent government, and everyone who held redbacks eventually felt that shortfall directly.

📚 PART 2 · LAND, THE ONE THING THE REPUBLIC HAD ENOUGH OF

With little cash and heavy debt, land functioned as the Republic's only real currency. Headrights, land grants offered to new settlers, became the promise that pulled immigrants across the Sabine River into Texas, since the government had abundant land even when it had almost nothing else to offer.

💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
This is the same pattern the whole quarter keeps returning to: when the Republic lacked one kind of resource, it leaned hard on whatever resource it actually had. Here, that resource is land itself.

In 1839, President Mirabeau Lamar moved the capital to a new town named Austin, sited deliberately at the edge of Comanche range. That location was not a retreat from Comanche territory; it revealed Lamar's actual intention to expand into it. Building a functioning government, currency, and land system from nothing, for a population that could not yet pay for any of it, was this Republic's defining act of responsibility, whether it succeeded completely or not.
