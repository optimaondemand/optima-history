📚 PART 1 · THE TERMS OF UNION

Tyler's 1844 treaty failed to win the two-thirds Senate vote a treaty required. The dispute was significant enough that it had to be settled through the presidential election that followed, after which Congress passed a joint resolution instead, requiring only a simple majority. That resolution passed in March 1845, and Texas formally accepted its terms that December. Texas kept its public lands and its existing public debt, and reserved the right to divide into as many as five states in the future, a concession specific to Texas's unique situation as a former independent republic.

💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
No other new state joined the Union this way. Keeping its own public lands, keeping its own debt, and reserving a right to divide into five states are all terms unique to Texas's situation as a former independent republic, not the standard path any territory takes to statehood.
