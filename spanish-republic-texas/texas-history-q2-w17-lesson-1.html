📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two presidents, two completely different theories of the frontier.
Sam Houston favored conciliation with Indian peoples on the frontier. Mirabeau Lamar favored expulsion instead. This lesson follows what Lamar's approach actually produced: escalating violence that peaks in a parley gone wrong and a devastating raid.

📚 PART 1 · TWO THEORIES, ONE FRONTIER

Houston believed conciliation, treating with Indian peoples and honoring agreements, offered the Republic's best path to peace. Lamar believed the opposite: that expulsion was the only realistic policy on a frontier this contested. Lamar's approach produced the Cherokee War of 1839, ending in the death of Chief Bowles and the removal of the Cherokee from East Texas. William Goyens, a free Black interpreter and negotiator active in East Texas Indian diplomacy throughout this period, worked to ease exactly this kind of conflict, with mixed results. A smaller uprising, the Córdova Rebellion of 1838, had already shown how unsettled this same frontier already was.

📚 PART 2 · A PARLEY, THEN A RAID

In March 1840, Texian officials invited Comanche leaders to San Antonio for a parley intended to negotiate the return of captives. When the Comanche delegation did not deliver the captives Texians expected, the meeting broke down into violence, remembered as the Council House Fight. That fall, the Comanche retaliated with the Great Raid of 1840: Victoria was struck, Linnville was sacked and burned, and Comanche riders reached the Gulf coast itself before being turned back at the Battle of Plum Creek.

⚠️ WATCH OUT
A parley is a negotiation, not an ambush by design. The Council House Fight matters because negotiations genuinely broke down over unmet demands, not because either side walked in already planning violence.

Mounted Comanche warriors were extraordinarily difficult for earlier firearms-based tactics to defeat: a skilled rider could loose roughly twenty arrows in the time it took a soldier to reload a single-shot firearm. That single fact explains almost everything about frontier combat in this era, and it sets up exactly the problem next lesson's revolver finally solves.
