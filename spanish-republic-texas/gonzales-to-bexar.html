📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A war begins, but not yet the war you might expect.
Fighting opens at Gonzales in October 1835 and moves quickly toward Béxar. But the colonists calling themselves to arms are not yet fighting to leave Mexico. They are fighting to restore the Constitution of 1824.

📚 PART 1 · A CANNON, A RIVERBANK, AND A TOWN
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before you read on: do you think a war can be fought seriously without either side having fully decided what winning would actually mean? (No wrong answer — just check in with yourself.)

On October 2, 1835, Mexican soldiers arrived at Gonzales demanding the town's small cannon back. Colonists refused, raising a flag reading "Come and Take It" instead. It is the first shot of the war. On October 28, Texian forces fought from a riverbank cutbank at Concepción, giving this course its first real look at what this lesson calls the cover principle: fighting from a protected position changes what a smaller force can accomplish against a larger one. By December, Texian forces had taken the town of Béxar itself, and the Alamo along with it.

📚 PART 2 · A WAR FOR 1824, NOT YET FOR SEPARATION

The Consultation of November 1835 declared for restoring the Constitution of 1824, not for independence from Mexico. Around the same time, Texian leaders planned the Matamoros Expedition, an attempt to link up with Mexican federalists further south who opposed Santa Anna's centralism. Nobody plans an expedition like that if the war is already understood as a war of separation.

💡 HELPFUL TO KNOW
Hold this fact carefully as you read the next lesson. What this war is actually about will change while it is still being fought, and the men holding the Alamo will very likely never learn about that change before the fort falls.
