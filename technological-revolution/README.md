# Technological Revolution

The wave of invention and applied science from the late 1800s into the early 1900s, with Thomas Edison as
the exemplar: electric power and light, the telephone and telegraph, new steel and chemical processes, the
internal combustion engine, and the people and laboratories that turned discoveries into industries. Covers
both America and Europe, since the inventions, the inventors, and the capital moved back and forth between
them.

A cross-cutting narrative folder like `industrial-revolution/`, not a chronological-era folder; see the
root README's note on the two kinds. It picks up where the industrial revolution's mills and factory system
leave off, and it overlaps in time with `gilded-progressive/`. A lesson can sit here even when another
folder's timeframe also reaches it. Where the overlap creates a real placement question, a lesson goes by
emphasis: a lesson mainly about the invention and its inventors belongs here, a lesson mainly about the
factory system and its spread belongs in `industrial-revolution/`, and a lesson mainly about the politics,
business, or reform of the era belongs in `gilded-progressive/`. As with every folder here, this boundary is
guidance, not a rule.
