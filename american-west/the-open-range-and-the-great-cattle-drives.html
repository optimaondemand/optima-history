A few words in this lesson are worth reviewing before you read. They are shown in bold with a dotted line. Hover over one to see what it means.
drover (DROH-ver): A person who drives cattle or other animals over a long distance.
maverick (MAV-er-ik): A calf with no brand. The word comes from Samuel Maverick, a Texas rancher who did not brand his cattle.
railhead (RAYL-hed): The end of a railroad line, where cattle and goods were loaded onto trains.
stampede (stam-PEED): A sudden, wild rush of frightened cattle.
trail boss (TRAYL BOS): The leader of a cattle drive.
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
In Texas, a steer was worth a few dollars. In a Northern city, the same steer was worth ten times as much. Someone had to close the gap.
After the Civil War, Texas had millions of longhorns and almost no way to sell them. The answer was one of the great journeys in American history: the long drives north. You will see why the drives began, how a trail crew lived, and what made them possible, from rivers and bridges to railroads and stockyards.

📚 PART 1 · THE DRIVES BEGIN

Closing the Gap

During the Civil War, Texas ranchers could not ship their cattle anywhere. Herds multiplied on the open range. By 1865, millions of longhorns roamed Texas, and almost nobody wanted to buy them. A steer sold for just a few dollars. In Northern cities, which had grown quickly, beef was in demand, and a steer might sell for ten times as much.

This is a simple lesson in supply and demand. Where something is plentiful and demand is low, the price is low. Where it is scarce and demand is high, the price is high. A person who could carry the cattle from one place to the other could earn a profit. The problem was the distance, and the lack of railroads in Texas.

The answer was to drive the herds north on foot to a railhead, a town at the end of a rail line, where cattle could be loaded on trains. The first big drives went to Sedalia, Missouri, in 1866. In 1867, an Illinois businessman named Joseph McCoy built stockyards at Abilene, Kansas, on a railroad, and invited Texas drovers to bring their cattle there. The route that led to Abilene became known as the Chisholm Trail, named for Jesse Chisholm, a trader who had marked part of the way. The trail ran north from South Texas, through San Antonio, Austin, Waco, and Fort Worth, crossed the Red River into Indian Territory, and ended in Kansas.

📚 PART 2 · ON THE TRAIL

Life on the Trail

A typical herd had about 2,500 to 3,000 cattle, and a crew of about ten or twelve men. The trail boss was in charge. Good cowboys rode on the sides and back of the herd, keeping it moving. The cook drove the chuck wagon, which carried food and supplies. Charles Goodnight is credited with inventing the chuck wagon in 1866. A boy or young man called the wrangler looked after the remuda, the spare horses.

💡 FOREIGN LANGUAGE VOCABULARY
remuda (reh-MOO-dah): The extra horses kept with a cattle drive, so riders could change to a fresh horse.

A herd traveled about ten to fifteen miles a day, and the trip to Kansas took two or three months. It was hard work. The riders faced river crossings, thunderstorms, and dry stretches with no water. At night, a lightning bolt or a loud noise could cause a stampede, and the cowboys had to ride hard in the dark to turn the herd before it scattered. Men were injured and killed.

The cowboys were a mixed group. Many were young white Texans, but a large share of them were Black and Mexican. Some had been enslaved before the war. Bose Ikard, a Black cowboy, rode with Goodnight on the trail for four years. Goodnight valued him as a trusted friend and put a tribute to him on his gravestone. The men earned about a dollar a day, and they were glad for the work.

📚 PART 3 · THE CATTLE TRADE

Rivers, Rails, and Stockyards

The drives needed roads and bridges. The Brazos River at Waco was hard to cross when it ran high. In 1870, the city opened the Waco Suspension Bridge. It was the longest bridge of its kind west of the Mississippi River at the time, and drovers paid a toll for each animal that crossed. Farther north, cowboys swam herds across the Red River.

The railroads themselves came closer. In 1872, the Missouri-Kansas-Texas Railroad reached Denison, on the Red River. Soon other lines entered Texas, and cattle could be loaded on trains much closer to the ranches. Fort Worth, a town on the trail, grew into “Cowtown.” In 1887, the Fort Worth Stockyards opened, and after 1902, large meatpacking plants were built there. The cattle trade became a business, and Texas beef went to markets across the country. Over time, some drives shifted west onto the Western Trail toward Dodge City, Kansas, as Kansas, worried about a tick-borne cattle disease called Texas fever, closed more of its land to Texas herds.

When the Law Was Weak

The open range made the drives possible. But open land had a downside. Cattle wandered, brands were hard to check, and a calf with no brand was a maverick. The word comes from Samuel Maverick, a Texas rancher who did not brand his herds. Anyone who found one could claim it. Many honest ranchers did. Some thieves used it as an excuse to take other men’s cattle.

In Mason County, in the Hill Country, quarrels over cattle exploded in 1875. Men were shot and masked groups hanged suspected thieves. The community split along family and ethnic lines, between German settlers and Anglo ranchers. The county courts could not stop it. The state sent Texas Rangers, and the killing slowed after a few years. At least a dozen men died. The lesson of the Mason County War is plain. When the means to settle disputes peacefully are missing, people settle them with guns.

Texas beef fed a growing nation, and Texans learned lessons about markets, bridges, and law. But the open range itself was not going to last. In a few years, a simple invention would begin to change it.

💪 WHAT TO CARRY FORWARD

The drives were one chapter of a story that stretched across the Great Plains, and Texas supplied the cattle. Ask what a long, dangerous job takes from the people who do it, and what a community needs to hold together when there are no walls.
